Part 1: Multiple-Choice Questions (20)

Choose ONE correct answer. The answer and a brief explanation are provided below each question.

1. Why do countries engage in international trade?

  • A. All countries have identical resources
  • B. Countries differ in resources, technology, skills, and preferences
  • C. Governments require every country to import everything
  • D. Trade eliminates all production costs
Correct answer: B. Countries differ in resources, technology, skills, and preferences
Explanation: Differences between countries create opportunities for specialization and exchange.

2. According to mercantilism, a nation's wealth was traditionally measured by its accumulation of:

  • A. Human capital
  • B. Gold and silver
  • C. Consumer goods
  • D. Foreign workers
Correct answer: B. Gold and silver
Explanation: Mercantilists believed accumulating precious metals increased national wealth and power.

3. What trade policy does mercantilism generally support?

  • A. Maximizing imports
  • B. Eliminating all tariffs
  • C. Maximizing exports and restricting imports
  • D. Importing more than exporting
Correct answer: C. Maximizing exports and restricting imports
Explanation: Mercantilism aims to generate a trade surplus by encouraging exports and limiting imports.

4. Who is known as the Father of Modern Economics and wrote The Wealth of Nations?

  • A. David Ricardo
  • B. Adam Smith
  • C. Eli Heckscher
  • D. Michael Porter
Correct answer: B. Adam Smith
Explanation: Adam Smith published The Wealth of Nations in 1776 and advocated specialization and free trade.

5. What is an absolute advantage?

  • A. Producing a good with a lower opportunity cost than another country
  • B. Producing more efficiently than another country using comparable resources
  • C. Producing every good at a higher price
  • D. Importing goods without paying tariffs
Correct answer: B. Producing more efficiently than another country using comparable resources
Explanation: Absolute advantage focuses on greater productivity or fewer resources needed per unit.

6. Cambodia can produce more rice than Japan using the same resources. In rice, Cambodia has:

  • A. A trade deficit
  • B. A comparative disadvantage automatically
  • C. An absolute advantage
  • D. No production advantage
Correct answer: C. An absolute advantage
Explanation: Greater output from comparable resources indicates an absolute advantage.

7. Who developed the theory of comparative advantage?

  • A. Adam Smith
  • B. David Ricardo
  • C. John Maynard Keynes
  • D. Milton Friedman
Correct answer: B. David Ricardo
Explanation: David Ricardo developed comparative advantage and explained it in his 1817 work.

8. Comparative advantage is determined by:

  • A. Absolute output only
  • B. Lower opportunity cost
  • C. Higher wages only
  • D. The size of a country's population
Correct answer: B. Lower opportunity cost
Explanation: A country has comparative advantage when it sacrifices less of other output to produce a good.

9. What is opportunity cost?

  • A. The money earned from exports
  • B. The cost of transporting goods
  • C. The value of the next-best alternative forgone
  • D. The total tax paid on imports
Correct answer: C. The value of the next-best alternative forgone
Explanation: Opportunity cost measures what must be given up to obtain or produce something.

10. Can two countries benefit from trade if one country has an absolute advantage in both goods?

  • A. No, never
  • B. Yes, if their comparative advantages differ
  • C. Only if both countries ban imports
  • D. Only if their production costs are identical
Correct answer: B. Yes, if their comparative advantages differ
Explanation: Different relative opportunity costs allow specialization to benefit both countries.

11. The Heckscher-Ohlin theory explains trade mainly through differences in:

  • A. Factor endowments
  • B. National flags
  • C. Exchange-rate systems alone
  • D. Government leaders
Correct answer: A. Factor endowments
Explanation: Countries tend to export goods that intensively use their relatively abundant factors of production.

12. A country with abundant capital is generally expected by Heckscher-Ohlin theory to export goods that are:

  • A. Capital-intensive
  • B. Always labor-intensive
  • C. Impossible to manufacture domestically
  • D. Unrelated to its factor endowments
Correct answer: A. Capital-intensive
Explanation: The theory predicts exports will use relatively abundant production factors intensively.

13. Which is an example of a factor of production?

  • A. A tariff
  • B. Land
  • C. An export quota
  • D. A trade surplus
Correct answer: B. Land
Explanation: Land, labor, and capital are common production factors; technology and skills also affect productivity.

14. What is a major problem associated with excessive protectionism?

  • A. Guaranteed lower prices
  • B. More competition in every industry
  • C. Higher prices and fewer choices for consumers
  • D. The complete elimination of trade conflicts
Correct answer: C. Higher prices and fewer choices for consumers
Explanation: Import restrictions reduce foreign competition and can raise consumer prices.

15. A trade surplus occurs when:

  • A. Imports exceed exports
  • B. Exports exceed imports
  • C. Exports equal zero
  • D. A country has no foreign trade
Correct answer: B. Exports exceed imports
Explanation: A trade surplus equals exports minus imports when the result is positive.

16. Which policy is commonly associated with mercantilism?

  • A. High tariffs on imports
  • B. Removing all trade restrictions
  • C. Prohibiting exports
  • D. Allowing imports without restriction
Correct answer: A. High tariffs on imports
Explanation: Tariffs and import restrictions were used to protect domestic producers and limit imports.

17. What is a central benefit of specialization and trade?

  • A. Both countries must produce all goods themselves
  • B. Resources can be allocated more efficiently
  • C. Opportunity costs disappear
  • D. All countries earn identical incomes
Correct answer: B. Resources can be allocated more efficiently
Explanation: Specialization directs resources toward activities where countries have a relative advantage.

18. Which statement best describes free trade?

  • A. All imports must be prohibited
  • B. International exchange faces fewer government-imposed barriers
  • C. Only the government may trade
  • D. Exports are always taxed at the highest rate
Correct answer: B. International exchange faces fewer government-imposed barriers
Explanation: Free trade reduces barriers such as tariffs and quotas, although regulations may still exist.

19. Which situation illustrates comparative advantage?

  • A. A country produces more of every good
  • B. A country specializes in the good with the lower opportunity cost
  • C. A country exports only goods it cannot produce
  • D. A country bans all foreign products
Correct answer: B. A country specializes in the good with the lower opportunity cost
Explanation: Comparative advantage depends on relative opportunity cost, not simply total output.

20. According to the central message of trade theory, countries can benefit by:

  • A. Avoiding all economic cooperation
  • B. Specializing and exchanging goods and services
  • C. Producing only goods with the highest prices
  • D. Restricting all international competition
Correct answer: B. Specializing and exchanging goods and services
Explanation: Specialization and exchange can increase total output and the goods available for consumption.

Part 2: True or False (20)

Decide whether each statement is True or False. The answer and explanation follow each statement.

1. International trade occurs partly because countries have different natural resources.
Answer: True
Explanation: Countries differ in land, minerals, climate, and other resources.
2. Mercantilism considers imports more important than exports for building national wealth.
Answer: False
Explanation: Mercantilism encourages exports and restricts imports to accumulate gold and silver.
3. Adam Smith is associated with the theory of absolute advantage.
Answer: True
Explanation: Absolute advantage emphasizes producing goods more efficiently than other countries.
4. David Ricardo developed the theory of comparative advantage.
Answer: True
Explanation: Ricardo showed that trade can benefit countries through differences in opportunity costs.
5. Comparative advantage depends only on which country produces the largest quantity.
Answer: False
Explanation: It depends on relative opportunity cost, not output alone.
6. A country can benefit from trade even if it has an absolute advantage in every good.
Answer: True
Explanation: It can specialize in the good for which its comparative advantage is strongest.
7. A trade surplus occurs when imports exceed exports.
Answer: False
Explanation: A trade surplus occurs when exports exceed imports.
8. High tariffs can reduce foreign competition and increase consumer prices.
Answer: True
Explanation: Tariffs raise the cost of imported goods and can limit consumer choice.
9. The Heckscher-Ohlin theory emphasizes differences in factor endowments.
Answer: True
Explanation: Differences in the relative availability of land, labor, and capital help explain trade patterns.
10. Mercantilism views international trade as potentially zero-sum.
Answer: True
Explanation: Its traditional view emphasizes one country's accumulation of wealth at another's expense.
11. Free trade always guarantees that every individual benefits equally.
Answer: False
Explanation: Trade may create overall gains, but the benefits and costs can be distributed unevenly.
12. Specialization can improve the allocation of productive resources.
Answer: True
Explanation: Countries can concentrate resources on goods they produce at relatively lower cost.
13. Opportunity cost is the value of the next-best alternative forgone.
Answer: True
Explanation: It measures what is sacrificed when a choice is made.
14. Under comparative advantage, a country should always specialize in every good it produces most efficiently in absolute terms.
Answer: False
Explanation: Specialization is guided by comparative advantage and lower opportunity cost.
15. Natural resources are the only reason countries trade.
Answer: False
Explanation: Technology, labor skills, preferences, climate, and other factors also influence trade.
16. Import quotas can restrict the quantity of goods entering a country.
Answer: True
Explanation: A quota places a limit on imports of a specified product.
17. Mercantilism generally favors unrestricted imports and no government intervention.
Answer: False
Explanation: Mercantilism supports government control and restrictions intended to encourage a trade surplus.
18. Countries with relatively abundant capital may specialize in capital-intensive goods under Heckscher-Ohlin theory.
Answer: True
Explanation: The theory predicts exports will tend to use relatively abundant factors intensively.
19. Trade can give consumers access to products that are unavailable or costly to produce domestically.
Answer: True
Explanation: Imports expand product availability and may lower costs through international specialization.
20. All countries must have identical production costs to benefit from international trade.
Answer: False
Explanation: Differences in opportunity costs are one reason mutually beneficial trade can occur.

Part 3: Questions & Answers (4)

Question 1. Explain mercantilism and its main principles.

Model answer:

Mercantilism is an early theory of international trade, prominent from approximately 1500 to 1776. It argues that a nation's wealth and power are increased by accumulating gold and silver.

Main principles:

  1. Maximize exports to earn revenue from foreign markets.
  2. Minimize imports to limit payments to other countries.
  3. Use government intervention, including tariffs, subsidies, quotas, and shipping regulations, to control trade.
Explanation: Mercantilism aims to maintain a trade surplus. Excessive protectionism may raise consumer prices, reduce efficiency, and cause trade conflicts.

Question 2. Differentiate absolute advantage and comparative advantage.

Model answer:

Absolute advantage, associated with Adam Smith, focuses on greater production efficiency or fewer resources needed per unit. Comparative advantage, associated with David Ricardo, focuses on lower opportunity cost.

Absolute advantage compares productivity; comparative advantage compares what must be sacrificed to produce a good. Comparative advantage explains how both countries may benefit even when one is more productive in both goods.

Question 3. Explain the theory of comparative advantage and why it benefits countries.

Model answer:

The theory states that a country should specialize in producing the good for which it has a lower opportunity cost than another country. Countries can export goods in which they have comparative advantage and import other goods. This allows resources to be used more efficiently and can increase total production and consumption. The key is relative opportunity cost, not simply producing more than another country.

Question 4. Explain the Heckscher-Ohlin theory of international trade.

Model answer:

The Heckscher-Ohlin theory explains trade through differences in countries' factor endowments, such as land, labor, and capital. A country tends to export goods that intensively use the production factors it has in relative abundance and import goods that intensively use relatively scarce factors. For example, a capital-abundant country may specialize in capital-intensive manufactured products, while a labor-abundant country may specialize in labor-intensive products.

Part 4: Case Studies (4)

Read each situation, answer the questions, then review the model answers and theory tested.

Case 1: Cambodia and Japan (Rice and Cars)

Situation: Cambodia can produce 100 tons of rice or 20 cars per year. Japan can produce 40 tons of rice or 120 cars per year using comparable resources.
  1. Which country has an absolute advantage in rice?
  2. Which country has an absolute advantage in cars?
  3. How can specialization benefit both countries?
Answers and explanations:
  1. Cambodia has an absolute advantage in rice because it produces 100 tons compared with Japan's 40 tons.
  2. Japan has an absolute advantage in cars because it produces 120 cars compared with Cambodia's 20 cars.
  3. Cambodia can focus on rice and Japan on cars, then exchange products. Specialization and trade can give each country access to more goods than under the same resource constraints alone.
Theory tested: Adam Smith's theory of absolute advantage.

Case 2: Cambodia and Singapore (Rice and Computers)

Situation: With 100 units of labor, Cambodia can produce 10 tons of rice or 2 computers. Singapore can produce 4 tons of rice or 8 computers.
  1. Calculate the opportunity cost of one ton of rice in each country.
  2. Identify which country has a comparative advantage in rice and computers.
  3. Recommend a specialization strategy.
Answers and explanations:
  1. Cambodia: $2\div10=0.2$ computer per ton of rice. Singapore: 8 computers correspond to 4 tons of rice, so one ton of rice costs 2 computers. Equivalently, one computer costs 5 tons of rice in Cambodia and 0.5 ton of rice in Singapore.
  2. Cambodia has a comparative advantage in rice (0.2 computer per ton versus Singapore's 2 computers). Singapore has a comparative advantage in computers (0.5 ton of rice per computer versus Cambodia's 5 tons).
  3. Cambodia should specialize in rice and Singapore in computers, then trade to improve consumption possibilities.
Theory tested: David Ricardo's theory of comparative advantage.

Case 3: A Country Restricts Imports

Situation: Country A introduces high tariffs on imported goods, subsidizes domestic exporters, and imposes quotas on certain imports. Consumers later face higher prices and fewer product choices.
  1. Which trade theory does this policy represent?
  2. What is the government's main objective?
  3. Identify two possible disadvantages.
Answers and explanations:
  1. Mercantilism, because the government uses restrictions and export subsidies to control trade.
  2. The objective is to increase national wealth and power by encouraging exports, restricting imports, and seeking a trade surplus.
  3. Possible disadvantages include higher prices and fewer choices for consumers, inefficient resource allocation due to reduced foreign competition, and possible trade disputes.
Theory tested: Mercantilism and protectionism.

Case 4: Different Resources, Different Exports

Situation: Country X has abundant capital, advanced machinery, and developed technology. Country Y has abundant labor and large agricultural areas. X exports machinery and industrial equipment; Y exports agricultural products and labor-intensive goods.
  1. Which theory best explains this situation?
  2. Why does Country X export machinery?
  3. Why might Country Y export labor-intensive goods?
  4. How can both countries benefit from trade?
Answers and explanations:
  1. The Heckscher-Ohlin theory.
  2. Country X has relatively abundant capital and advanced technology, supporting capital-intensive production.
  3. Country Y has relatively abundant labor and agricultural resources, which can support labor-intensive goods.
  4. Specialization and exchange give X access to agricultural products and Y access to machinery, improving resource allocation and expanding choices.
Theory tested: The Heckscher-Ohlin factor-endowment theory.

Final Exam Cheat Sheet

  • Mercantilism: Export more, import less, accumulate gold and silver, and use government intervention.
  • Adam Smith - Absolute Advantage: Produce a good more efficiently than another country.
  • David Ricardo - Comparative Advantage: Produce a good at a lower opportunity cost.
  • Heckscher-Ohlin Theory: Trade patterns depend on differences in factor endowments, such as land, labor, and capital.

Important Formulas

  • Trade balance = Exports - Imports.
  • Trade surplus: Exports > Imports.
  • Trade deficit: Imports > Exports.
  • Opportunity cost of one unit of $A =$ Maximum output of B $\div$ Maximum output of A, assuming the same resources and a straight-line production possibility frontier.
